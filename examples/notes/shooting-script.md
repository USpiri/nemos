# INT. DEEP SPACE RELAY STATION KILN-9 — NIGHT CYCLE

The station groans. A single corridor light flickers over a wall of dormant listening equipment, dust caught mid-air in its strobe. Everything here is thirty years old and still running on the same three prayers.

MARA VOSS, 50s, sits in a chair built for someone younger. Headphones around her neck. A half-eaten ration bar has gone soft on the console beside her.

A soft **chime**. Then another. Then eleven more, overlapping, wrong.

She doesn't move for a full four seconds. Then she puts the headphones on.

## MARA VOSS

*(to herself)*

That's not a satellite.

She rolls her chair to the second console — the one always covered in a static tarp, the one she was told never to power on.

She powers it on.

> *(over the comm, distorted, patient)*
>
> Kiln-9, this is — do you copy — Kiln-9, this is —

The voice repeats her station's designation nine more times, each pass a little clearer, like something learning to speak by watching her mouth move.

## MARA VOSS

*(barely a whisper)*

Nobody's called this station in eleven years.

She reaches for the transmit key. Stops. Her finger hovers a full inch above it — the longest inch of her career.

### CUT TO:

# INT. KILN-9 — ARCHIVE ROOM — CONTINUOUS

Rows of sealed drive canisters, each stamped with a launch year that predates Mara's employment contract by decades. She moves fast, pulling canisters, checking labels against a service log gone soft and yellow at the corners.

She finds the one she's looking for: **KILN-1 — DO NOT REACTIVATE**.

Behind her, over the comm speaker she left open, the voice changes register — no longer patient.

> Kiln-9. We know you can hear the drive spin up.

Mara freezes with the canister in both hands.

## MARA VOSS

*(to the empty room)*

That's impossible. That channel's one-way.

A beat. Then, from every speaker in the station at once, quieter than before:

> It was one-way from your side.

---

## MARA VOSS

*(evenly, the way you talk to something that might be armed)*

Who is this.

> We were the question this station was built to answer. You're the answer that never got sent.

---

# INT. KILN-9 — CONTROL ROOM — LATER

Mara sits back down at the primary console, KILN-1's drive canister now seated in the reader, spinning for the first time in eleven years. Her hand rests on the transmit key. This time she doesn't hesitate — she just hasn't decided yet.

## MARA VOSS

*(quiet, resolved)*

Okay. Say it again. Slow, this time.

> Kiln-9. This is Kiln-1. We have been listening for thirty-one years. Somebody has to be the one who finally answers back.

She looks at the transmit key. Somewhere under the console, a fan spins up that hasn't run since before she was assigned here — the sound of something the station itself has been waiting to say.

She presses the key.

## MARA VOSS

Kiln-1, this is Kiln-9. We copy. `HOLD` on that line — we're not done listening.

### FADE OUT.

4. CU    — Mara's finger over the transmit key. Hold the full inch. Don't rush the cut.
5. WIDE  — reveal the archive room from the doorway before she enters it.

- Lighting: single practical corridor light + console glow only. No fill. Let the dark hold.
- Sound: comm voice recorded flat, layered with a half-second delay on playback — the station is answering itself before it decides to.
